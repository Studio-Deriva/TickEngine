================================================================================
TICKENGINE - HOW WE VERIFIED AND FIXED THE BUGS
================================================================================
Project : TickEngine 1.0.0-beta.1, updated for 1.0.0-beta.2
Stack   : Minecraft 1.21.11 / Fabric loader 0.19.5 / Fabric API 0.141.6+1.21.11 /
          Mojang mappings / Java 21
Bundled : fabric-permissions-api 0.6.1
Scope   : the whole mod - profiler, block entity and entity throttling,
          auto-tuning, config, commands, HTML reports, ServerCore integration

This is the record of the audit TickEngine went through before its first
public beta, of what changed because of it, and of how much the mod actually
saves. 7 findings, all closed. It is published for two reasons.

The first: TickEngine changes how the game runs wherever no player is
looking. Machines run slower, mobs think less often, and auto-tuning can lower
the view distance of everyone online. Anyone running it on a server deserves
to know how hard that was looked at, and what it costs in gameplay.

The second: the last section says what has NOT been verified. Every audit has a
boundary. Ours is written down instead of implied.

Nothing here is marketing. Where a fix or a measurement has a known limit, the
limit is stated next to it.

Severities are the ones used during the audit:

  CRITICAL  item duplication, mass data loss, or the server's authority bypassed
  HIGH      a concrete exploit: crashing the server, locking players out of an
            area, losing data
  MEDIUM    a real defect with limited reach or a harder trigger
  LOW       a real defect with minor impact
  INFO      a problem only in unusual setups, or with no gameplay impact

No CRITICAL or HIGH finding came up. No item duplication path was found.


================================================================================
PART 1 - HOW THE CODE WAS EXAMINED
================================================================================

What the findings rest on:

  * Full reads of every source file: the mixins into block entity and entity
    ticks, the throttles, the profiler, auto-tuning, config, commands, the
    HTML report, ServerCore detection and admin notices.

  * The decompiled Minecraft 1.21.11 sources, not documentation. They settled:
    that Mob.serverAiStep alternates a full goal selection and a
    running-goals-only update on the parity of tickCount + id (finding 2.1);
    that ElderGuardian and Warden fire their area effects on
    (tickCount + id) % 1200 and % 120; that item and XP despawn is driven by a
    private age field reaching 6000; that beacons, vaults and trial spawners
    act only on game times divisible by 80 or 20; that DedicatedServer's
    distance setters write server.properties while PlayerList's do not.

  * The ServerCore 1.5.15+1.21.11 jar, to read its activation range and
    dynamic settings flags through reflection without depending on it.

  * Every mixin validated against 1.21.11 before building.

  * The Fabric dev server (./gradlew runServer) with a flat test world, driven
    from the console: 256 hoppers, 256 furnaces, cows and pigs in
    force-loaded chunks. Furnace output, item despawn, tick counts and rule
    precedence were checked against expected numbers.

  * A real client (./gradlew runClient) connected to the dev server, for what
    needs a player: the AI zone, admin notices on join, the click-to-teleport
    in /tickengine top, done in survival.

  * A real Fabric server (official launcher, not the dev environment) with
    -Xmx3G and 17 other mods: Lithium, FerriteCore, ServerCore, worldgen mods,
    mob and AI mods, block entity mods (Simple Copper Pipes, CC: Tweaked).
    67 mods loaded counting libraries.

  * A player's own modpack on a local server with two clients, including
    Physics Mod, under loads built to push the server past 50 ms per tick.

What was NOT used: no debugger session (the IDE bridge was not available),
no run on the reference hardware. C2ME was not run during the audit; it was
checked later, see PART 5.


================================================================================
PART 2 - THE 7 FINDINGS AND THEIR FIXES
================================================================================

Ordered by severity.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 [MEDIUM] Half of the throttled mobs never started or stopped a goal
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the throttled zones a mob's AI runs only on ticks where
tickCount % interval == 0. Vanilla Mob.serverAiStep picks the kind of goal
update with i = tickCount + getId(): when i is odd it only ticks the goals
already running (tickRunningGoals(false)), otherwise it runs a full selection.
With an even interval - 4 by default, 2 in force-loaded chunks, 10 and 20
under auto-tuning - tickCount is always even on the AI turn, so the parity of i
is the parity of the id. Every mob with an odd id got the partial update
forever: no new goal, no goal stopped.

In practice, about half of the far mobs never ate grass (sheep never grew their
wool back), never picked a target, never fled, never followed food, until a
player came close.

Reproduced: 40 sheared sheep on grass in force-loaded chunks, no player,
180 s. Throttle on: 22/40 with wool. Throttle off: 40/40. Halving the AI rate
alone would give about 80%; 55% fits half of the flock never starting the
eating goal.

FIXED - throttle/EntityThrottle.java, mixin/MobMixin.java. A throttled entity
on its turn now ticks in a mode of its own, and for mobs the partial update is
replaced by a full goal selection. Same test after the fix: 38/40. With a
player nearby, 20 sheep per zone over 180 s: 17/20 in the AI zone, 18/20
beyond.

The same parity problem hits ElderGuardian (mining fatigue on
(tickCount + id) % 1200) and Warden (darkness on (tickCount + id) % 120),
inside the AI step itself, and a goal fix can't reach it. Both are now never
throttled, by two default rules ("ai": 1, "tick": 1) that a server can remove.

Limit: default rules are written only into a new config file. A config created
before this version keeps its own rule list.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 [MEDIUM] One syntax error in the config file wiped every setting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If the file failed to parse - a double comma, a missing brace - the exception
was logged and save() ran anyway, writing out the values in memory. At startup
those are the defaults, so the whole config, rules included, was replaced by
defaults. On /tickengine config reload, the previous values were written back
and the edit with the typo was lost. The log line ("using defaults for
unreadable values") suggested a per-option fallback; it was the whole file.

Reproduced: defaultInterval 10 and a rule "minecraft:hopper": 8, then
"sampleEveryTicks": 20,, and a restart. After it, the file had
defaultInterval 4 and no hopper rule.

FIXED - config/TickEngineConfig.java. A file that can't be parsed is left
untouched and the settings already in use stay (the defaults at startup). The
log says so and where the error is; /tickengine config reload fails with a
message instead of claiming success. Checked: same broken file, same hash
before and after both a restart and a reload; after fixing the typo, the
reload loads the hopper rule and the interval.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 [LOW] output.reportDirectory could write reports anywhere on the machine
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The value was resolved against the server folder without normalising it, so
absolute paths and ".." were accepted, and missing folders were created. A
user with tickengine.config - operator level 3 by default, or a LuckPerms node
given to staff who have no file access - could make the server create folders
and tickengine-<date>.html files wherever its process can write. File name and
content are not under their control, so the reach is small.

Reproduced: ../../../../<outside path>, then /tickengine profile report; the
file appeared outside the server folder.

FIXED - config/TickEngineConfig.java, report/HtmlReport.java. The option
refuses absolute paths and paths that leave the server folder once
normalised. The report writer checks again before writing, in case a value
gets in another way. Checked: "../../escape" and "/tmp/escape" refused,
"tickengine/../tickengine/reports" accepted.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 [LOW] Bare /tickengine showed the status to users without tickengine.status
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The root command accepted any of the status, top, profile or config
permissions and ran the status directly, while "/tickengine status" needed
tickengine.status. With separate LuckPerms nodes, someone with only
tickengine.top saw MSPT, throttle state, the auto-tuning level and admin
notices.

FIXED - command/TickEngineCommand.java. Bare /tickengine shows the status only
with tickengine.status; otherwise it lists the subcommands the user may run.

Not reproduced with a player during the audit: it needs a permissions mod to
grant nodes separately. Checked after it with LuckPerms 5.5.21 (PART 5).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 [LOW] Teleporting from the ranking could still land a player inside a block
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For block entities the landing point was the block's position plus one,
without checking it was free. With stacked machines (hoppers under furnaces,
rows of machines on several levels) the admin landed inside the block above
and suffocated in survival.

FIXED - command/TickEngineCommand.java. The landing point is the first spot,
from the block above (or the entity's position) up to 64 blocks higher, with
two free blocks. Checked with a player in survival on a hopper at y -60 under
a solid block at -59: the click landed at -58, with -58 and -57 free.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6 [LOW] Auto-tuning thresholds were not checked against each other
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
raiseAboveMspt and lowerBelowMspt were only range-checked one by one. With
lowerBelowMspt at or above raiseAboveMspt and an MSPT in between, the level
went up on one decision and down on the next, forever: a simulation and view
distance change every 10 seconds, chunks resent to every client.

FIXED - config/TickEngineConfig.java, tuning/AutoTuner.java,
command/TickEngineCommand.java. "/tickengine config set" refuses a value that
breaks lowerBelowMspt < raiseAboveMspt and keeps the previous one. A file
with such values loads with a warning, and auto-tuning does not relax while
they stay inconsistent. Checked: 50 for lowerBelowMspt and 20 for
raiseAboveMspt both refused.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.7 [INFO] The "without AI" counter included entities that have no AI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the AI zone every non-exempt entity got the AI-skipped mode and was counted
as such, including items, XP orbs, minecarts and armor stands, which have no
AI to skip and tick fully. /tickengine status overstated the work saved. No
gameplay effect.

FIXED - throttle/EntityThrottle.java. Only mobs get the AI-skipped mode; other
entities in the AI zone count as full ticks.


================================================================================
PART 3 - WHAT CAME UP WHILE TESTING
================================================================================

  * Auto-tuning flip-flopped near the threshold. Under a real load of about
    69 ms it went from level 0 to 2 in 20 seconds and brought the MSPT down to
    about 27 ms. Then it relaxed to 0 at once, the load came back to 45-51 ms,
    and it went up again: a cycle of about a minute. Harmless while only
    intervals change, unpleasant once view distance is involved.

    FIXED - tuning/AutoTuner.java, new option autoTuning.relaxAfterSeconds
    (default 60). It relaxes only after the MSPT has stayed low that long. If
    the load comes back within twice the wait, the next wait doubles, up to
    10 minutes, and goes back to the configured value after 10 calm minutes.
    0 keeps the old behaviour. Checked with thresholds set between two levels
    on purpose (13 and 12 ms, with level 2 at about 13.3 ms and level 3 at
    about 11.8 ms): one relax and one raise, the wait went to 120 s, and the
    level then held for about 7 minutes. The old code was not rerun with the
    same thresholds; the comparison is with the cycle seen before.

  * When the load sits close to players, the first levels (intervals) can't
    reduce it, and auto-tuning keeps climbing until it reaches the radii and
    the distances. With test thresholds it reached level 9 and lowered the
    view distance from 8 to 6. That is what the rules say it should do; the
    README now tells admins how to keep their distances.

  * Physics Mod showed no cost on the server: 148 client mixins and 2 server
    mixins. Its physics runs on the client, where TickEngine does nothing.
    The profiler groups time by the type that ticks, so work a mod adds to a
    vanilla entity through mixins is counted under that vanilla type.

  * The first click-to-teleport, found with a real client, put the player
    inside the block entity itself. Fixed before the audit; finding 2.5 is
    what was left of it.


================================================================================
PART 4 - WHAT TICKENGINE SAVES, MEASURED
================================================================================

All runs on a Mac, not on the reference hardware (an old 2-core PC with 4 GB).
The server got -Xmx3G; where noted, -XX:ActiveProcessorCount=2 so Java sizes
its thread pools and GC as on 2 cores. The core itself is much faster than an
old PC's, so the milliseconds are lower than they would be there. The A/B
comparisons are what matter. Order A, B, A, B in every test; A = TickEngine
disabled, B = defaults.

  1. Real Fabric server, 17 mods without C2ME, ActiveProcessorCount=2.
     512 hoppers passing items, 256 furnaces smelting, 500 mobs (cows, sheep,
     villagers, guard villagers, mooblooms, chickens), all in force-loaded
     chunks, no player. 60 s warm-up, 180 s measured with /tick query every
     5 s, plus the Java process CPU time.

       run   avg MSPT   P95 (median)   P99 max   CPU in 180 s
       A1    11.44 ms   14.4 ms        56.5 ms   45.7 s
       B1     7.24 ms    9.9 ms        26.9 ms   29.9 s
       A2    10.66 ms   13.2 ms        28.7 ms   44.0 s
       B2     6.94 ms    9.5 ms        25.8 ms   28.9 s

     Average MSPT -36%, P95 -30%, CPU -34%.

     This is the "farms kept loaded" case: in force-loaded chunks the default
     never slows things below one tick in two.

  2. Player's modpack, two players online. 256 hoppers, 128 furnaces and
     1400 mobs (600 villagers) in force-loaded chunks, one player about five
     chunks away. A also had auto-tuning off. 30 s warm-up, 90 s measured.

       run   avg MSPT          P95 (median)   CPU in 90 s
       A1    56.0 ms (17.9 TPS)  78.7 ms      91 s
       B1    38.0 ms             86.3 ms      68 s
       A2    49.6 ms             68.8 ms      88 s
       B2    24.5 ms             52.9 ms      49 s

     Average MSPT -41%, CPU -35%. Without TickEngine the server used a whole
     core, fell below 20 TPS and logged "Can't keep up!"; with it, it held
     20 TPS. The load eased a little over time (A2 < A1, B2 < B1), which is
     why the two pairs differ. P95 was mixed: worse in B1, better in B2.

  3. Same modpack, lighter: 450 mobs, same machines.

       A: 16.3 and 17.1 ms   B: 12.6 and 11.6 ms   (average MSPT -27%)
       P95 median: A 21.1 and 24.1 ms, B 27.2 and 26.1 ms

     Here P95 got worse with TickEngine. Our guess, not verified: with
     blockEntities.stagger off, every throttled block entity ticks on the same
     ticks, so less total work lands on fewer, heavier ticks. stagger true
     spreads it out, at the cost of the game-time guarantee above.

What it costs in gameplay, by design: far machines run at 1/4 speed (1/2 in
force-loaded chunks without players), far mobs react later. In the sheep test
of 2.1, 17-18 of 20 far sheep regrew their wool in 180 s, where all 40 did
without throttling.

  4. Measured again with 1.0.0-beta.2, Java 21 (October 2026). Real Fabric
     server, Fabric API, Lithium and FerriteCore, ActiveProcessorCount=2 and
     -Xmx3G. A flat world built once and copied before every run: 512 hoppers
     moving stone between barrels, 256 lit furnaces, 1500 mobs (600
     villagers, 400 cows, 200 sheep, 150 chickens, 150 pigs) in one glass pen,
     all in force-loaded chunks, no player. Each run restarts the server, so
     every mob is loaded at the same moment. 60 s warm-up, 180 s measured,
     two rounds, variants interleaved.

       variant                          avg MSPT       P95 (median)   slowest P99
       no TickEngine                    12.56 12.57    15.9 15.7      38.4 26.6
       installed, everything off        12.71 12.56    15.8 16.0      34.0 26.7
       light profiler only              11.88 12.56    15.7 16.1      28.2 49.4
       defaults                         12.80 12.20    17.8 20.0      40.9 32.9
       defaults + tick spreading        12.44 12.24    16.5 16.5      27.4 33.2

     The cost of the mod itself, installed and off or with only the light
     profiler, is within the noise. The run-to-run spread is about 0.7 ms.

     The defaults saved nothing on the average here, against -36% in test 1.
     Throttling worked as configured: 50% of block entity ticks and 50% of
     entity ticks skipped (force-loaded chunks never go below one tick in
     two). But a mob's throttled turn cost about 1.6-1.7 times a normal
     tick (villagers 24 µs instead of 14.5 µs). With a temporary counter
     (not in the released jar), the paths computed per 10 s went from about
     750 to about 650 when only a quarter of the mob ticks ran: much of what
     these mobs do is paced by game time, not by how often they tick. Path
     timeouts, a suspect at first, were 0-3 per 10 s with throttling on or
     off.
     Raising forcedChunkMaxInterval to 4 brought the average down by about
     10-30% on the same world. A guess, not verified: test 1 ran 0.4.0,
     before the fix in 2.1, when half of the far mobs only updated the goals
     they were already running, which is cheaper.

     Tick spreading took the P95 from 17.8-20.0 ms to 16.5 ms, close to the
     server without TickEngine, and the slowest P99 down in one round of two.
     The average did not change.

PART 5 - WHAT IS STILL NOT VERIFIED
================================================================================

This is the honest boundary of the work. None of the following is a known
defect, unless it says so; all of it is something nobody has watched happen.
Updated for 1.0.0-beta.2.

  * The reference hardware. Every measurement ran on a Mac. The relative saving
    should be similar on an old 2-core PC; it has not been measured there.

  * Long runs. No server stayed up for more than a few hours. Memory growth
    over days, and behaviour across many restarts with players on, have not
    been watched. Chunk unloads and reloads with throttled entities were
    checked (the same 270 entities back, throttling resumed), not over days.

  * Other servers. The beta has not been run by anyone outside the studio
    yet.

  * Dense farms in force-loaded chunks. In the beta.2 measurement (PART 4.4)
    the defaults did not lower the average there. Whether other layouts
    behave the same, and how much of it comes from the full goal selection
    of 2.1, has not been measured.

  * Tick spreading on modded mobs and machines. It moves each entity's tick
    counter once after loading. Vanilla does not wait for exact counter
    values there; mods were not checked one by one.

  * Mods with their own chunk loaders. Areas they keep loaded with their own
    tickets are treated as far chunks, not as force-loaded ones. That is known;
    how each such mod reacts has not been tried.

  * Modded machines and mobs beyond the tested set (Simple Copper Pipes,
    CC: Tweaked, Guard Villagers, Friends&Foes, Mutant Monsters, Oh The Biomes
    We've Gone; on the Forge port, a 252-mod pack). Create does not exist for
    Fabric 1.21.11 and was not tested there. Logic in other mods that relies
    on ticking every tick, far from players, may slow down or misbehave; a
    rule with interval 1 turns throttling off for it.

  * Spark. Measurements used /tick query, the process CPU time, TickEngine's
    own profiler and Java Flight Recorder, not spark.

Checked since beta.1, and no longer on this list:

  * C2ME 0.4.0-alpha.0.28 on Java 25 (Temurin 25.0.5), with Lithium and
    FerriteCore: no error or warning involving TickEngine, 377 new chunks
    generated in the Overworld and the Nether while throttling, chunk unloads
    and reloads with throttled entities. C2ME's own warnings about optional
    mods it did not find (Starlight, ScalableLux, native math on macOS) are
    unrelated.

  * The Nether and the End on a real Fabric server: piglins, zombified
    piglins, hoglins, magma cubes, endermen, shulkers and endermites, hoppers
    and furnaces, all throttled; per-dimension rules switch throttling off in
    one dimension and back on when removed.

  * Permissions with LuckPerms 5.5.21: granted and denied nodes, the fall
    back to the operator level, admin notices only with tickengine.notify.

  * The cost of the mod itself: installed with everything off, or with only
    the light profiler, it is within the noise (PART 4.4).

  * The P95 guess in PART 4.3: the cause is entities loaded together sharing
    their throttled turn, not block entity stagger. Tick spreading addresses
    it (PART 4.4).

If you run TickEngine on a server and find something this audit missed, tell
us: https://discord.gg/pQPWr7vfHu. A reproduction with the steps, your mod
list and an HTML report from /tickengine profile start is worth more than any
amount of further reading.
